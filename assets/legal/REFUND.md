# Refund Policy for Screw Out

**Policy Version: 1.0**  
**Effective Date: October 16, 2026**  
**Last Updated: October 16, 2026**

All purchases in Screw Out are made through **Google Play** or the **Apple App Store**. Payments are handled by the store, so refunds are issued by the store under its own policy. We will gladly help if something went wrong.

## What you can buy

- **Screw Out Pro** — monthly or yearly auto-renewing subscription
- **Remove ads** — one-time purchase
- **Coin packs** — consumable virtual currency
- **Theme packs** — one-time cosmetic unlocks

## Requesting a refund

**Google Play (Android)**  
- Within 48 hours of purchase you can usually request a refund yourself at https://play.google.com/store/account/orderhistory or through Google Play's help centre.  
- After 48 hours, email us (below) with your order ID (it starts with `GPA.`) and we will review your request.

**Apple App Store (iOS)**  
- Request a refund at https://reportaproblem.apple.com. Apple decides on App Store refunds; we cannot issue them directly.

## Subscriptions

- You can cancel Pro at any time in Google Play → Payments & subscriptions, or in your Apple ID subscription settings. Cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep Pro until the end of the period you paid for.
- We do not offer partial refunds for unused time in a subscription period, except where required by law or where a technical problem prevented you from using Pro.

## Coins and other virtual items

- Coin packs are consumed when they are added to your balance, so they are generally non-refundable once delivered, except where required by law.
- If a purchase was charged but the coins, theme or Pro benefit did not arrive, first try **Settings → Restore**. If it still hasn't arrived, email us with your order ID and we will deliver the item or support a refund.
- If a refund is granted, the related items (coins, themes, Pro, Remove ads) are removed from your account.

## Your statutory rights

Nothing in this policy limits rights you have under the consumer laws of your country (for example, the EU/UK right of withdrawal where it applies to digital content you have not yet started to use).
